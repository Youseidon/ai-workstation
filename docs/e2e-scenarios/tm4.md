## Scope and fixed decisions

TM4 adds handover on an existing item thread.
A requester who needs help finishing a work item captures it, pushes a feature branch and opens an offer in the item thread; a teammate accepts, checks out the branch, works on it with their own workstation, their own settings, their own provider login and their own quota, and hands the item back.

**The lifecycle is not redefined here.**
[protocol.md](../telegram-task-control/protocol.md) section 5 is authoritative for every state, authorized actor and required condition, and H02 builds that table rather than a summary of it.
An earlier draft of the handover rules invented a six-state machine and a `RELEASED` state and was withdrawn; no row below reintroduces either.

**The offer is an open call.**
It names no receiver, any available teammate may accept, and the first accept wins by the shared update rather than by which tap reached a bot first.
B17, R-A, RTC-11 and protocol.md were all amended on 2026-09-20 to match.
The control record is therefore the arbiter of a simultaneous accept, which is why H02 precedes H03 and why the record must exist before an offer does.

**There is no release action and no `RELEASED` state.**
A receiver who cannot finish uses `return_work`, the result is labelled partial, and that is what releases the executor.
TM4's migration adds exactly seven actions: `publish_offer`, `accept_offer`, `decline_offer`, `withdraw_offer`, `return_work`, `apply_result` and `request_changes`.
No row below asserts an eighth.

**Neither Git record exists yet.**
`refs/aw/items/<item>/control` and `aw/handover/*` are referenced nowhere in `server/src`; the only Git-carried record built today is the roster `refs/aw/team`, and `item_link.control_head` is a placeholder column that no caller ever writes.
Every repository assertion below is therefore a new-behaviour assertion, not a regression one.

TM4's migration is **number 29 or later**: F02 took 27 and F07 took 28.

**The T0 scenario mapping follows the burn-down brief, settled by jd on 2026-09-21.**
TM-T0-6 is the control record and its lifecycle, which H02 builds, and TM-T0-7 is capture, preview and publish, which H03 builds.
[engineering-plan.md](../telegram-task-control/engineering-plan.md) lines 455 to 456 previously defined the two the other way round, as snapshot capture and apply, which the brief's H02 and H03 rows contradicted.
The plan was amended to match, so there is no longer a conflict to rediscover.
Nothing is lost by the brief's mapping: the apply behaviour the plan had put in TM-T0-7 is asserted at the T1 tier by TM-T1-H1 for a clean apply and TM-T1-H3 for conflict and idempotent re-apply, while the plan's mapping would have left H02 and migration 29 with no T0 row at all.

TM4 excludes, deliberately, so no row assumes them: a third team member, further handoff beyond a return to the requester, remote Stop across workstations, package signing and device keys, and any hosted relay.
A receiver returning partial work is a return to the requester, so it stays inside the boundary; a requester stopping a receiver's run from their own phone is remote Stop, so it does not.

The accepted defaults stay unchanged, and handover adds one.
A Team action expires after 10 minutes.
Grants end when handover starts.
The shared-record read interval is the recorded 5-second default.
**An offer expires after 24 hours**, and expiry moves it from `OFFERED` to `WITHDRAWN`.
This follows [protocol.md](../telegram-task-control/protocol.md) rather than the Proposed row in the handover rules that said an offer never expires on its own: protocol.md gives the Offer record a start deadline, lists expire on that transition, and defaults its approval deadline to 24 hours.
The 24-hour value matches that approval default; it was the orchestrator's proposal and jd accepted it with the ruling of 2026-09-21, so it is a chosen default rather than one jd named unprompted.
Expiry cannot affect a claim that already exists, and what does not expire is the requester's freedom to publish a fresh offer.
The 10-minute action expiry remains a property of the card, not of the offer.
`team.enabled` stays false by default and these scenarios enable it explicitly in both environments; handover stays behind its own disabled capability until G02's evidence half, RTC-12's capability matrix, is produced inside H04.
This table deliberately does not choose the capability's setting key, a control-record polling interval other than the recorded default, or a credential-shape detector.

The F track's fixes are load-bearing here and rows assert against them rather than around them: a closed item refuses every action (F02), a supergroup upgrade repairs itself before anything else is processed (F05), anchor writes are bounded and compared on material content (F07), and a completed anchor renders from the completed state and retires only once its run has ended (F08).

### Settled: the control record's ref namespace

**The control record lives at `refs/aw/items/<item>/control`, a custom ref outside `refs/heads/`. jd settled this on 2026-09-21.**

Two authoritative documents had disagreed.
[protocol.md](../telegram-task-control/protocol.md) section 4 gave the layout as `aw/control/<task-id>`, an ordinary branch under `refs/heads/`, while [teammate-design.md](../telegram-task-control/teammate-design.md) section 6, [handover-rules.md](../telegram-task-control/handover-rules.md), the H02 task row and G04 in [implementation.md](../telegram-task-control/implementation.md) section 6d all gave `refs/aw/items/<item>/control`.
These are different namespaces with different host-protection behaviour, so the choice changes what the compare-and-swap rests on, what the host's branch protection covers, and what G04's retention rule points at, and H02 could not build both.
protocol.md was amended after the ruling, so it no longer contradicts.

The evidence behind the ruling, recorded here because H02 builds on it.

- teammate-design.md section 6 never offered the two as alternatives. It names the custom ref as primary and makes the ordinary branch `aw/control/<item>` a **contingency**, reached only "if it does not hold", where what must hold is the host rejecting a non-fast-forward update of a ref outside `refs/heads`.
- That condition was tested and passed. LG-1, recorded as H-TM-LG1 on 2026-09-17, pushed and fetched `refs/aw/*`, had a divergent non-fast-forward update rejected and triggered no Actions run. Its human-verification row states the conclusion in terms: this permits the compare-and-swap rather than falling back to ordinary branches. **The contingency therefore never fired**, and `refs/aw/team` has shipped on that same evidence since TM1.
- protocol.md section 4 keyed its layout to `<task-id>`, which predates TM2's item identity, while every later document keys the record to `<item>`, which is what `item_link` and the group tags are built from.
- The ordinary branch would also put handover state inside the product branch namespace the remote protects for code, which G04's "keep the records, delete the branches" rule deliberately separates.

## Observable layers

- **Phone/group:** the offer card in the item thread naming no receiver, the receiver's own bot posting its Accept and run card, the capture preview with its file list and flagged credential shapes, anchor progress, the loser's inert card and the reason it gives, completion and review cards.
- **Transport:** each bot's delivered updates and Bot API calls under LT-1 broad administrator delivery, receipts answered promptly, edits coalesced, and no output from a workstation that owns nothing.
- **Durable:** migration 29 schema, the seven actions, action references and applied-once receipts, `item_link` with `control_head` written and read, local task and run rows, and the caches that must defer to the record.
- **Repository:** the `aw/handover/<item>` branch, the control record's `state.json` and `events/<command id>.json`, single-parent commits, fast-forward or rejection only, no force-push ever, and no token, path or credential in any ref.
- **Execution:** exactly one workstation runs the item at a time; the receiver runs in its own worktree under its own provider login and its own approved policy; a refusal starts nothing and leaves no orphan worktree.
- **Browser:** the handover surface stays hidden while the capability is disabled, personal controls remain available, and the requester's pipeline hold is visible until the work is applied or the handover ends.
